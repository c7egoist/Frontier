Can you make a coin
hover without tipping?

Try an active stabilizer: it senses the coin's position and tilt, then moves and tilts the magnet underneath. Nudge the coin, try to flip it, or steer the hovering point in 3D.

!

This is active magnetic levitation—not a normal coin floating over one fixed magnet. The simulated coin is magnetized, with like poles facing. A passive magnet arrangement can't stably hold it in every direction (Earnshaw's theorem); the moving base uses feedback and actuators, which need external energy. An ordinary coin usually isn't a permanent magnet at all.

What makes this 3D setup different?

Vertical support alone is not enough. Repelling magnets can support an object in one direction while it drifts sideways or tips in another. The simulation gives the lower magnet an XY carriage, a height stage, and a gimbal; a feedback controller moves them to correct those errors.

This is a simplified teaching model, not a full magnetic-field calculation. The coin, magnet force, control law, and actuator energy are illustrative; a real levitator needs sensors, a fast controller, powered actuators, and careful safety limits.

Try these tests

Watch both the coin and the red-topped magnet carriage. A correction is an action by the powered controller—not free energy from the permanent magnet.

01

Switch the stabilizer off

Press “Nudge coin”. The stationary repelling setup lets the coin drift away. Its vertical motion can look supported while sideways stability is lost.

02

Challenge the coin's tilt

Press “Try to flip it”. With feedback on, the base magnet gimbal moves to correct the tilt; with feedback off, the simulated coin tips over.

03

Steer it across the stage

Move the X target slider. The controller shifts the base magnet so its repulsive force nudges the hovering coin toward the new position.
