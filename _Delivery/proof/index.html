Frontier — rendering proof

Every image, GIF and MP4 here was produced in this sandbox by CPU reference renderers that read the project's own headers. Nothing is a screenshot of the Vulkan app. The GPU side is simulated on the CPU.

Read this before you trust any pixel here.
  • Project-Zero — ReSTIR stills come from the project's own CPU ReSTIR path tracer. The raster and SDF-GI images come from the new Project-Zero-PathCompare, a CPU mirror: the raster path is a z-buffer with the engine's light list, the SDF path marches a DistanceAtlasSpace volume I bake. Shading is my path tracer, not the engine's material model. Scene, geometry, camera and light directions are the project's.
  • Project-Drive — course from DriveCourse.h::EmitCourseTriangles, body/rim/tyre meshes from the generated ControlVehicle*.inl, pose from the real VehicleSolver. Shading is mine; geometry and dynamics are the project's.
  • No GPU, no Windows here. The code fixes are compile/syntax-checked only.
The finding from the tumble. On a hard landing the soft tyre puts 998 kN through one wheel in a single 240 Hz step — 313× the static wheel load — with 407 nodes in contact, 156 mm of penetration and the rim-flange bottoming constraint handed 159 mm. The per-node XPBD contact λ stays at 0.0016, so the force is the sum of raw λ/h² over ~400 nodes with no clamp. The control run (identical drive, no spin) still peaks at 29.7 kN = 9.3×. See _Delivery/PROOF.md section 4 and the CSVs in Project-Drive/.

The vehicle tumble — GIF and MP4

The car is driven by the real VehicleSolver over the real course. At t = 4.30 s the scripted drive has it leave the ramp at 11.26 m/s; body rates (2.5, -7.0, 1.2) rad/s are injected there so it tumbles. The CONTROL clip is the byte-identical drive with no spin.
